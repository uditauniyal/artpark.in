Our approach

The breakthrough is only the beginning.

A research result becomes a successful venture only when a team keeps aligning a consequential problem, a differentiated solution, a viable business model and the capabilities to deliver — inside an ecosystem that makes that work affordable.

The breakthrough is necessary. It is nowhere near sufficient.

02 · The objective

We judge translation by one test: products used and paid for at scale.

A prototype, a patent or a funding round is a milestone on the way, not the outcome. The test does not prescribe a vehicle: some products become startups, some use nonprofit models, and some have government as the long-term customer — which still counts as being paid for.

Products

Something a user can acquire and rely on — not a paper, a patent or a demonstration.

Used

Real users, in the environment where it must work — not a product built but never adopted.

Paid for

A market, or a government customer, that pays — not indefinite grants as the only income.

At scale

Reach large enough to matter to the problem — not a pilot that never spreads.

03 · The Venture Translation Framework

Five dimensions align around the founding team, inside an ecosystem.

A change in any one dimension forces changes in the others. The founding team keeps them aligned, and the ecosystem supplies capabilities the venture cannot yet own.

ECOSYSTEM · labs, test environments, early customers, industry, capital, regulation
MARKET AND CUSTOMER

Whose problem, in which segment, is urgent enough to pay to solve?

Evidence: a named user, buyer and budget owner; a bottom-up count of the first segment; a paid pilot.

TECHNOLOGY AND PRODUCT

Does it work under the conditions that matter to this customer?

Evidence: field performance, reliability data, integration trials, independent validation.

BUSINESS MODEL

Who pays, for what, and at an acceptable margin?

Evidence: bill of materials, service cost, utilisation, pricing tests, unit economics.

FOUNDING TEAM AS INTEGRATOR

Connects the five and makes the trade-offs.

Recruits the missing capabilities and surfaces inconvenient evidence.

TEAM AND CAPABILITIES

Who can make it real, and which capabilities are missing?

Evidence: hires made, partners committed, delivery milestones met.

ALTERNATIVES AND COMPETITION

What is the customer comparing it to — including doing nothing?

Evidence: better total cost or outcome than today’s process, import or workaround.

The founder as integrator

Someone must keep connecting the five dimensions — whether or not they perform each function themselves. The qualities we look for:

Curiosity First-principles depth Systems thinking Critical judgement Problem-solving stamina Resourcefulness Learning agility
Hustle alone is not enough; unfocused persistence amplifies a bad assumption. The valuable form is evidence-seeking execution.
04 · What we believe

The work between research and real-world use is not the paperwork after innovation. It is part of innovation itself.

  1. 01

    A working result is where the questions start.

    Whether a city could afford electric light was a separate question from whether the lamp glowed.

  2. 02

    Test it where it will live.

    Real environments add requirements no lab result satisfies alone.

  3. 03

    Not every result should become a startup.

    Some should be licensed, some become open infrastructure, some need a consortium or a public customer.

  4. 04

    The unglamorous work is invention too.

    Integration, reliability, manufacturing and economics deserve the same respect, funding and talent as the original insight.

06 · A diagnostic for venture reviews

Questions, not scores.

Beyond the five dimensions, these are the questions that expose the real risk in a venture review. We use questions, not scores, until a score has been calibrated against a real portfolio.

IP and defensibility

Which rights are secured? What advantage comes from know-how, data or certification?

Industrialisation

Can it be manufactured, tested, installed and maintained repeatedly?

Adoption

Who is the first credible customer, and what blocks repeat purchase?

Capital

Which uncertainty will the next tranche retire? Is the instrument right?

Ecosystem

Which capabilities must come from outside, and are they available in time?

Governance

Are IP, licensing, founder roles and conflicts settled early and transparently?
