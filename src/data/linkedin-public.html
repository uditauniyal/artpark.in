About us

ARTPARK @ IISc (AI & Robotics Technology Park) fosters innovations in AI & Robotics by bringing together the best of research, startup, industry, and government ecosystems. We drive mission-oriented deep-tech innovation projects leading to technology business incubation in areas such as industrial automation, mobility, agriculture, healthcare, security and education. ARTPARK is seed-funded by the Department of Science & Technology (DST), Govt. of India, under the National Mission on Interdisciplinary Cyber-Physical Systems (NM-ICPS) and the Govt. of Karnataka.

    More nonprofits are building AI agents for health, education and agriculture. But how do you know an agent works reliably before a health worker, farmer or student starts depending on it? As part of Pragati AI for Impact, the^delta and ARTPARK, Indian Institute of Science (IISc) are hosting Calibrate: Testing and Improving AI Agents in Non-Profits, a practical masterclass on evaluating and improving agent performance. Led by Jigar Doshi, ML Director, and Aman Dalmia, Principal ML Engineer, at ARTPARK, the session will show how their team tests its own agents and how yours can do the same. The session will cover: • Testing before and after deployment, including common mistakes to avoid. • Creating evaluations and strengthening them with harder test cases and real-world failures. • Integrating testing into development and monitoring quality once agents are in use. • Using Calibrate with AI tools such as Claude to turn evaluation results into specific fixes. You’ll also see a live demo of Calibrate, ARTPARK’s open-source evaluation tool, to help you get started.     Aman Dalmia from ARTPARK, IISc spoke at IndiaFOSS 2026, one of the largest gatherings of India’s open-source community, about Calibrate. Calibrate is an open-source tool for evaluating AI systems, used by nonprofits in India and Africa across education, agriculture and health to make sure their AI products work reliably. Using a voice agent that fills public health forms as a case study, the talk walks through how to evaluate AI systems so they can be trusted: Writing unit tests for the different scenarios an agent needs to handle The challenge of evaluating free text, and how LLMs can be used to evaluate other LLMs Comparing models on quality, cost and speed to find a deployable configuration Preventing regressions and catching mistakes before they reach end users Continuously monitoring an agent’s quality in production and adding errors back to grow the evaluation dataset Watch the full talk: https://lnkd.in/gsrcZMAm FOSS United #IndiaFOSS #OpenSource #AIEvaluation

    We finally sat down to write up some of our work, and I'm pleasantly surprised by my statistical luck: of the five papers we submitted, four were accepted at NeurIPS 2026 workshops. What surprised me even more is how much reviewers appreciated careful, rigorous experimental work done before and after deployment, in the messy real world. At SAHAI, our group at ARTPARK (IISc), we're building AI systems for frontline health workers. Together these papers cover three things: support on the job, removing digital drudgery, and structured learning. 🩺 "Scaling and Evaluation Across India: A High-Risk Pregnancy Chatbot for Community Health Workers." Sthitapragyan Parida, Saumitra Sharma, Sanskriti M., Jigar Doshi NeurIPS 2026 Workshop on GenAI4Health. This is our most mature and scaled system. Through our partners ARMMAN, it reaches 1 to 1.5 million pregnant mothers every year. The paper looks back on two years of systematically evaluating it in the field. 🛑 "Semantically Identical, Clinically Opposite: A Negative Result on Semantic Caching in a Deployed Maternal-Health Assistant." Saumitra Sharma, Sthitapragyan Parida, Jigar Doshi. NeurIPS 2026 Workshop on GlobalSouthAI. So many incoming questions looked alike that we thought, why not just match them to known answers, fast and cheap? We spent six months on it and then walked away, because we just couldn't make it safe enough. I love that the research community is open to this: this negative result got our highest-rated reviews. 🗣️ "Model Selection for Hindi Form-Filling Voice Agents Under Noisy Speech, Cost and Latency Constraints." Aman Dalmia, Sanskriti M., Jigar Doshi. NeurIPS 2026 Workshop on GlobalSouthAI. India's 2.5 million frontline health workers each spend 60 to 90 minutes a day filling in apps and forms. Adds up to about a billion hours a year, taken from a workforce that's already overworked. FormBharo is our attempt to give that time back by letting them fill forms once by voice. It's now in pilot with ARMMAN for antenatal care (ANC) visits. Full paper: https://lnkd.in/g9XHEJ-B Project Page: https://lnkd.in/g9WiDr6p 🎭 "Too Agreeable to Teach: Multi-Resolution Evaluation of Simulated Learners in AI-Facilitated Training." Chandan S Patil, Anugraha Raman, Jigar Doshi. NeurIPS 2026 Workshop on UserSim. With CMHLP (Pune) and Atmiyata, we're piloting an AI mental-health trainer where an AI facilitator teaches alongside simulated peer learners. What we found is that simulated peers are too nice (not exactly sycophantic). The class seems to learn faster than a human one, but the AI is really just pushing the task forward while the learners don't actually progress. The lesson for us: if you want to evaluate learning, you have to track where the evidence comes from and whether each step forward is real. All of this wouldn't have been possible without our incredible partners, funders and artpark team. Tagging them all below in chat and links to paper coming soon #NeurIPS2026

    A proud moment for all of us at ARTPARK. QOSMIC has built India’s own Fast Steering Mirror and taken it through thermal vacuum and vibration qualification on the first attempt, nine months after starting from scratch. Only a few companies in the world make space-grade FSMs, so India has had to import them. With QOSMIC manufacturing one here, our optical and quantum communication missions now depend a little less on foreign suppliers for a critical component.     QOSMIC

    4,993 followers

    Nine months ago, this did not exist. Today, India can build its own Fast Steering Mirror. QOSMIC’s Fast Steering mirror has cleared thermal vacuum and vibration qualification, both passed on the first attempt. The FSM is the fine-pointing core of every optical link, satellite to ground, satellite to satellite or ground to ground. It steers light with precision, holding a laser locked on target while everything around it moves, making hundreds of tiny corrections every second. To put that precision in perspective: lay a grid over Rashtrapati Bhavan and the estate around it. From orbit, our FSM is designed to have the ability to point at every 100 m from 500 km above your head to any single point on that grid. With only two or three suppliers worldwide with this class of hardware, it was essential to QOSMIC to indigenise this critical piece of the puzzle.   From nothing to space-qualified, TRL 7, in under a year. Built to survive launch.
